What DoctaRx Adds

DoctaRx does not replace hospitals, doctors, PHCs, pharmacies, or government systems. It adds a digital coordination, teleconsultation, reporting, and visibility layer around the patient journey.

The Centre of This Presentation

DoctaRx Abuja Pilot Program

A small, measurable, low-risk pilot to connect patient care, video consultation, referral follow-up, pharmacy visibility, forecasting insight, and public-health reporting.

Pilot Scope

  • 2–3 selected Primary Healthcare Centres
  • 1 General Hospital
  • Participating doctors
  • Participating pharmacy partners
  • Video consultation support
  • Referral tracking workflow
  • Ministry / Department oversight dashboard
  • Aggregate reporting readiness + monthly reporting
  • 3-month active launch window inside a broader 12-month plan

Pilot Actors

  • Patient
  • PHC nurse / officer
  • Remote doctor
  • Pharmacy partner
  • Referral hospital officer
  • Ministry dashboard user
  • Health information / reporting officer
  • DoctaRx technical support

Pilot Workflow — first request to follow-up

    Why this is low-hanging fruit: The pilot is small enough to approve, practical enough to implement, useful enough to measure, clear enough for non-technical officials, and scalable after proof — because it adds a layer rather than replacing anything that already works.

    Video Consultation Support

    Remote doctor consultation support for selected PHCs and patients — extending access while keeping doctors and facilities central.

    Demo Facility View · Sample Pilot Data
    • Selected PHCs and patients connect with doctors remotely.
    • PHC officers can assist patients where digital literacy is low.
    • Doctors review the patient summary during the consultation.
    • Doctors document notes, then issue a prescription or referral.
    • Video consultation extends clinical reach without replacing hospitals.

    No 24/7 national coverage is promised. This is not emergency-care replacement.

    Data Analysis & Forecasting for Better Planning

    Trend visibility and forecasting support for planning — simple dashboards help decision-makers see trends early and plan better.

    • Aggregate, non-identifiable dashboard trends.
    • Common symptom categories & facility activity.
    • Medicine demand patterns & referral load.
    • Response time & follow-up completion.
    • Seasonal trends where appropriate (e.g. rainy-season fever/malaria).

    Responsible language only: “forecasting support”, “trend visibility”, “planning insight”. No perfect-prediction or official-surveillance claims. No real patient data.

    Fever / malaria-like symptom trend Sample Pilot Data

    NHMIS / DHIS2-Ready Reporting Support

    Aggregate reporting views aligned with NHMIS/DHIS2-oriented requirements — reporting readiness, not a replacement for existing government systems.

    What this means

    • The platform can prepare aggregate reporting views.
    • Dashboard fields can be aligned to national reporting needs.
    • Facility-level activity can be summarized.
    • Reports can be exported / structured for health information teams.

    DoctaRx is DHIS2-ready. It does not claim full DHIS2 integration, automatic official submission, or Ministry certification.
